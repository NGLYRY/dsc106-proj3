Finals Frenzy: When Do Students Feel the Most Pressure?

Students see the highest BPM levels at the start and 30 minutes before the end of the exam.

Start of Exam End of Exam Time: 08:40:00

WRITE UP

During final examinations, students experience varying levels of stress as indicated by their heart rates. Our study reveals two critical moments of peak stress:

  • At the beginning of the exam, when students first encounter the questions
  • Approximately 20 minutes before the end, as time pressure intensifies

However, for the duration of the exam, the heart rate stayed at a steady rate between 80 to 90 bpm.

For our visualization, we decided to use data that tracked the heart rate of 10 students throughout the duration of a final exam. This Data is taken from PhysioNet, as cited below, of wearable bodily trackers in real world testing settings. We transformed the data to clean the trackers' messy data through filtering for the relevant final testing time period that needed to be measured and imputing missing values. To be able to compare each student and their levels of heart rate, we chose a radial graph as it succinctly illustrates all the students’ data simultaneously. For our primary interactive feature, we implemented a play/pause button that visualizes the levels of heart rate across the exam period. Clicking play begins the animation and users are able to pause at any moment. Additionally, there is a slider that allows the user to jump around and see the data at any given moment. On the graph, we included a hover feature; by hovering along the radial plot, users can see the exact beats per minute of each student at a certain time. Visually, the heart at the center also changes the pulse speed based on the mean heart rate at a given period, with an annotated text below for the specific BPM. To help the user draw the insights, we added an annotation on the slider that marks the time when students start to see an increased heart rate.

Initially, we wanted to visualize both temperature and heart rate at the same time, with temperature being shown through color. However, we determined it was better to focus on heart rate only as it was the most representative of student stress levels during a given exam period. Moreover, the addition of temperature could make the graph visually confusing to read. In terms of features, we initially planned for the slider as the main interaction along with the changing BPMs. We improved on this initial visualization by: (1) incorporating a play/pause button for easier user interaction, (2) animating the heart in the center of the visualization to match the mean heart rates of the students to illustrate stress levels at different times throughout the exam, and (3) an annotation towards the end of the exam to showcase the timeframe during which the students became more stressed.

Overall, we spent around 10 hours in total, with the aesthetics and animation features taking the longest time.
